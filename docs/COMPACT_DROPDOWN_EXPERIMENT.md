# Compact Player Experiment

Branch: `codex/compact-dropdown-experiment`

Baseline: `97e97238b2d449ad9a5b52938d3dfc5e14f4d8cc` on `codex/public-beta`.
Status: approved and frozen by the owner on September 16, 2026. This branch is stacked on `codex/public-beta` (PR #1). It is the accepted compact UI checkpoint, not a public-release readiness claim or a change to request routing or Spotify playback commands.

## Scope

- Dropdown and detached player width: 340 points instead of 400. Settings retains its existing layout.
- Smaller outer insets, player artwork, typography, section gaps, and composer padding. The connected landing page retains its centered VibeCast title and three inspirations; PiP sits beside the song details. Onboarding and developer view retain their heading. Settings, quit, developer toggle, and connection status live in a native right-click menu, with Contact Us. No playback controls were removed.
- Shared `PlayerDensity` environment controls layout only. `PlayerPresentation` selects the same compact metrics for both containers. No duplicate player implementation or separate playback state.
- Lyrics and queue open their reading modes directly. One `ReadingPlayerView` owns the compact artwork/song/artist/PiP header, flexible central viewport, and fixed seek/transport/device controls below. The old embedded pages, extra focus state, sparkles toggle, hint, and pulse effect are removed. Requests remain on landing and developer views only; draft text survives all transitions. The composer retains 14-point side insets and 16-point bottom clearance.
- Normal and miniplayer lyrics share an 18-point layout font in both containers. Inactive lines draw at 97% scale, growing gently to full size when active with a faint white glow in dark mode. This does not change wrapping or scroll anchors. Mini inactive lines use 40% white to restore contrast over artwork. Scrolling, top-to-center following, seeking, and floating sync placement are unchanged.
- Icon hit targets remain at least 28 points; the seek bar keeps its existing hit area and keyboard support.
- The native controller and shared view use one set of natural measurements across both containers. Resizing still happens before publishing the allocated content height, preserving the anti-flutter behavior.
- Mode changes and content measurements request sizing through the shared root view. A retired controller observer could overwrite a fresh request with its cached startup height when landing and miniplayer became equal-sized; removing that competing trigger preserves immediate startup transitions as well as per-mode resize policies.
- Detached lyrics and queue modes resize vertically, down to 344 points, matching the two-row desktop widget reference. Each retains its own height while switching screens, until redocking clears both sizes. Their dropdown versions remain 544 points, capped by the available screen. Landing, devices, developer view, and miniplayer remain user-size-locked while automatically fitting content. Width never changes; panel and draft state survive container switches.
- Album artwork toggles miniplayer in either container, including from normal reading modes. Exiting miniplayer returns to landing; devices opens its normal panel. Miniplayer lyrics and queue retain their separate, fixed-size screens with a shared metadata/PiP header and active lyrics/queue exit button. Their state is independent of normal panels and survives container switches. Artwork exit clicks are absent in either mini reading screen; its active button returns to the miniplayer via the shared ripple.
- Immersive is now the only miniplayer. The former Default layout, style model, Settings picker, save plumbing, and old plain-background tint branch are removed. Startup discards the retired style preference without changing other settings or accounts. Immersive is an edge-to-edge 340-point square with no artwork inset. Metadata, playback controls, PiP, and the bottom-weighted dark fade remain visible without hovering. The upper artwork through the artist row exits with the existing ripple; the seek/transport area, 52-point PiP corner, and detached-only eight-dot drag grip are excluded from the exit target. Playback errors remain visible below the artwork. Normal detached lyrics and queue modes have a noninteractive bottom-right resize hint; both mini reading screens remain size-locked without a resize hint.
- Miniplayer reading backgrounds use a 14-point blur on the cover only, overscanned to avoid transparent edges, plus a 62%-black veil with a subtle left-weighted fade. Queue secondary text is brighter over artwork while inactive lyrics remain dimmer than the current line. The queue embeds the same PlayerDetailsView and QueueScrollBehavior with its measured viewport, an independent activation counter, and the heading Next Up. No duplicate refresh, history, playback, or drag/snap implementation exists. Reopening and refresh start at Next Up; track changes preserve the current section and reuse the existing immediate refresh and history-growth fixes.
- Ripples retain the live content's identity at both endpoints using a stable clip shape. The completed clip derives its full rectangle from current bounds, preserving tall-window resizing without an offscreen mask or rebuilding the screen. Album covers share a bounded decoded-image cache (20 images / 40 MB), coalescing in-flight loads and supplying warm artwork synchronously on the destination's first frame. Decoding is off the main actor; failed loads keep the existing placeholder.
- A shared artwork accent samples a 32-pixel thumbnail off the main actor. The playback card uses a 30%-opacity fill above its material, consistent on landing, lyrics, and queue. Its bounded in-memory palette cache avoids repeated decoding. Missing artwork keeps a neutral mini background and the card's default teal tint. This does not invoke AI or change Spotify playback.
- Reading screens use the existing stable ripple transition directly from landing and between panels; Reduce Motion uses shorter fades. Native sizing remains unanimated and top-anchored to avoid reintroducing the old dropdown flutter. Queue positioning happens during native layout, and the initial network refresh does not perform a second scroll reset.
- The right-click menu uses a screen-coordinate anchor below the status icon, including on secondary displays. Local subscription status is restored at startup independently of Spotify, with a neutral checking state until the account is known. Visible menu status updates when the check or sign-out completes.
- Reading headers have a 0.5-point divider at 8% primary opacity, including the mini reading screens. Mini headers have three additional points of clearance below the artist row, balanced against the outer bottom inset to preserve the reading viewport and five-row history fit. Divider-to-content spacing is unchanged. Next Up and Recently Played have matching button-height header rows, aligning their top spacing without changing scroll anchors. Queue refresh remains next to Next Up; the floating lyric-sync button retains bottom clearance. The active lyrics/queue playback button returns to landing.
- Queue includes up to five previously observed songs, oldest first, above Up Next. This is session-local history, not a full Spotify listening-history import: it requires no new scope, survives panel/container changes, and clears on logout or relaunch. Polls and shuffle/pause/seek changes do not add duplicates; confirmed track changes do. Opening and manual refresh reset to Up Next. New history rows preserve the upcoming viewport instead of moving it into history.
- Both lists share numbered song rows, spacing, artwork, durations (`--:--` when unavailable), and one guarded playback handler. Upcoming selections step forward; history selections step backward. Each step is device-bound and confirmed before continuing. A mismatch stops the operation, with no blind retry or playback-context replacement. History is locally observed, not an authoritative Spotify backward queue, so this remains provisional pending the deferred direct-jump work.
- A narrow AppKit scroll marker uses the measured Up Next header as its anchor. Browsing within Up Next remains native. Pulling upward from Up Next or downward from Recently Played has matching 0.30 resistance and a 90-point threshold; releasing early returns to the originating section. Passing the threshold snaps to the other section with a 260ms ease-out. Fast scrolling from deeper in Up Next stops at its heading and requires a fresh pull to reveal history. Stops within 36 points of Up Next snap to it. Momentum alone cannot change sections; Reduce Motion disables snap animation. The marker and local event monitor are detached with the view.
- Queue anchoring observes the marker's ancestor frames as well as the document and viewport. A clamped scroll target remains pending until the document is large enough, so adding the first through fifth history rows cannot leave Up Next partially displaced. Section selection follows deliberate pulls, never a transient layout offset. Regression coverage samples opening and growth frame by frame in both containers, with empty and populated upcoming lists, and checks both snap directions at every history size plus refresh during a snap.
- Track changes restart the visible queue's refresh task immediately, even during a confirmed player command. Same-track shuffle/pause updates do not restart it. Playback polling uses two seconds while queue or either lyrics view is visible; the 12-second queue refresh remains a fallback for same-track edits. Cancelling/replacing a queue task retains the existing generation checks against stale results.
- Settings activates again after menu tracking ends and accepts activation clicks, including through SwiftUI on macOS 15+. Its close button uses the shared hover style. The request field now reads "Let's cast your vibe!", starts unfocused, and leaves editing when another control is clicked or its window resigns key status.
- Short reading windows let history scroll natively when all five rows do not fit. Crossing the last history row continues into resistance within the same gesture, without requiring a second pull. The trailing separator/gap is excluded from history overflow so fixed-size mini history keeps its original single-pull return. Growing the window clamps history's viewport back inside its section. Larger windows retain the original symmetric pull-and-snap feel.
- The composer has a clearer neutral border when idle, a teal border while editing, and brighter text with a left-to-right glow sweep on hover. Its animation clock exists only while hovered, runs at most 30fps, respects Reduce Motion, and does not change layout or focus. The retired Lyrics Mode hint's pulse implementation is removed.

With the local playback fixture, landing is 340 x 430 points, including the VibeCast title and all three arrow-free inspirations: Long Drive Night, Need to Focus, and Play Something Energetic. Inspirations and playlist recommendations now share an explicit 22-point gap below playback, without an empty request-status row adding extra space. The composer keeps its bottom and side clearance. Miniplayer and its reading screens stay 340 x 340; normal lyrics/queue default to 340 x 544. Content still grows for long titles, multiline input, recommendations, errors, and developer logs.

## Verification

- Automated coverage includes subscription restoration without opening Settings, live menu status updates, chronological bounded history, queue scroll restoration with an empty upcoming list in both containers, history growth without a viewport jump, pull/snap boundaries, shared forward/backward confirmation guards, and initial composer focus. Native tests additionally check Settings activation and first-mouse acceptance. Physical trackpad feel and real Spotify history stepping still require manual acceptance.
- Render light/dark player, lyrics and queue modes at multiple heights, mini reading screens, device picker, recommendation, onboarding, and developer states with `VIBECAST_RENDER_UI=1 ./script/swift.sh test --jobs 2`.
- Run native anchoring, dismissal, shared-host/state, and resizing tests with `VIBECAST_TEST_PRESENTATION=1 ./script/swift.sh test --jobs 2 --filter PresentationTests`.
- The suite checks long-title/multiline-request geometry, composer visibility rules, native menu order and conditional connection indicators, artwork palette extraction, and selective resizing. Native tests assert equal natural sizes on detachment and preserve the same hosting controller, modes, and draft through repeated miniplayer and Lyrics Mode round trips.
- Transition regressions check content lifetime at opening/closing endpoints, complementary clips, first-frame artwork reuse, cancelled outgoing views, and both reading modes' metadata and controls after resizing from 344 to 1,000 points. Native tests sample size and anchor throughout repeated landing/reading/miniplayer round trips in both containers and assert independent remembered lyrics/queue heights. Queue UI tests cover both normal and mini viewports, history sizes zero through five, first-song insertion, rolling history, both pull directions, short-window history browsing, reopen/refresh, and immediate confirmed/external track updates.
- Compare dropdown and pop-out manually, including long titles and requests, scrolling/seeking lyrics, entering/exiting Lyrics Mode, and returning to the dropdown from a resized window.
- With another app active, open the dropdown and check that its app menu/window appearance stays active; hover controls, click a control once, and type into the request field without submitting. Check Escape, outside clicks, another status icon, and Cmd-Tab dismissal. Repeat after redocking.
- Check that opening the dropdown does not highlight the progress bar before hovering it. Verify click/drag seeking and deliberate keyboard navigation still work in dropdown and detached modes.
- On macOS 27+, reveal an auto-hidden menu bar and open VibeCast from its status icon. Move into the player: the native expanded-interface session should retain the menu bar. Close with the icon, Escape, an outside click, or detach; system auto-hide should resume normally. Verify right-click/Control-click menus and ordinary always-visible menu bars too. The automated tests exercise delegate lifecycle/cancellation, not WindowServer's fullscreen tracking. Programmatic redocking has no public session-start API and retains the prior anchoring behavior.
- Leave the detached player on Desktop 1, switch to Desktop 2 or a fullscreen app, and click VibeCast's menu-bar icon. macOS should visit the player's original Space and raise it without moving the window onto the current Space. Check the same-Space and minimized-window cases as well. Native tests verify window policy and frame preservation; a real multi-Space session is needed to verify the desktop transition.
- Offscreen snapshots and native geometry tests do not replace checking perceived flicker and compositor effects on the actual desktop.
